From Tarpon Street to the sand

Mesa started as a small breakfast-and-lunch cafe in town, with good coffee and a line for the pancakes. It grew into a bistro, then walked half a mile north of the bridge and set its tables on the beach. Same kitchen habits, more sky.

Three slips down the dock

Walk it the way we did. Each post is a place Mesa tied up for a while. We have not pinned the years down yet, so they are marked until the owners confirm them.

  1. Tarpon Street

    Mesa Cafe · Vilma Linda Plaza · [year opened TBD]

    It began in town as Mesa Cafe, in Vilma Linda Plaza on Tarpon Street. Breakfast and lunch only. The coffee was the real thing, brewed strong, and people came back for it before work and after the dive boats.

    The pancakes built the following. The coconut French toast made it loyal. That plate came with us down the dock, and it is still the one people ask about.

  3. The Beach

    Mesa Cantina San Pedro · north of the bridge · [year moved TBD]

    Then the move to the sand. Mesa Cantina sits on the beach about half a mile north of the San Pedro bridge, close enough for a golf cart, easy by water taxi. The tables face the reef.

What we believe, in plain words

Good food on an island starts with the island. We cook European, Mediterranean and island-leaning dishes, and we build them around what is local and fresh on the island. If it is not good this week, it is not on the plate this week.

Vegetarians eat well here. Not one sad salad at the bottom of the menu, but real choices at breakfast, lunch and dinner. Bring the friend who does not eat meat. They will order seconds.

Coffee is not an afterthought. It was the reason people found us on Tarpon Street and it is still poured strong and hot from 8 in the morning.

Nobody is rushing you. You came a long way to sit by the sea. Stay for another coffee, another cocktail, another hour of watching the boats run the channel. The table is yours until you are ready.
